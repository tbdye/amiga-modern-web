# Page pixels: lossless ratios by coding and format

Scope: lossless compression ratios of real web pages' pixels by class of content, for nine codings in 16-bit, dithered 16-bit and 32-bit formats, on whole first screens, first-screen strips and scroll strips.
Status: current

- [R] Every figure here is this project's own measurement, made on 2026-10-11 on a Windows 10 PC with Microsoft Edge 154.0.4258.37, headless, from the eleven pages of `topics/page-pixels.md`; the same method on fresh captures would verify any of them.
- Every statement below is `[R]` on those terms.
- Captures, pixel formats, units, codings, the pages of each class and the conditions of each page are in `topics/page-pixels.md`, "Method" and "Pages measured"; ratios by page are there under "By page, 16-bit", and JPEG is in `topics/page-pixels-lossy.md`.
- A ratio is raw bytes ÷ coded bytes: higher is smaller, and under 1 the coded unit is larger than raw.
- Each cell is byte-weighted; median (range): the bytes on a link follow the byte-weighted figure, and the median describes a typical single unit.
- A 32-bit ratio is against 4 raw bytes a pixel and a 16-bit one against 2, so equal ratios mean twice the bytes at 32-bit.
- The pools are over the three window sizes together; ratios by window size were measured and are not carried.
- The class names are loose labels: "Text" includes a news front page full of photographs, which sets its low end, and "Application" pools pages too unlike for one figure to describe them, so read it beside the figures by page.
- The Application pool leaves out the signed-out YouTube home page, which is nearly empty; no pooled page is Discord's application or a YouTube page full of thumbnails.
